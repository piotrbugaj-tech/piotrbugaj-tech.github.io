Approach

Character and calm.

An interior has nothing to pretend. It only has to stay itself in the photograph and give the viewer enough air to slow down. Pictures like that are looked at, not scrolled past. And whoever arrives there later will recognise every corner.

  1. 1Sun and cloud

    Low, hard morning and evening sun draws out texture and depth; the soft light of an overcast day quietens everything. Each shows your interior from a different side.

  2. 2Flash on request

    Have a particular effect in mind? I can recreate it with flash and light modifiers.

  3. 3Detail and signs of life

    I arrange the flowers, move a vase, leave something on the table that says someone was here a moment ago. When a space needs more, I work with a home stager.

  4. 4A walk through the space

    Wide view, close-up, mood: one frame leads you on through the place, while straight verticals and calm geometry let the eye rest. A warm, natural edit ties it all into one story.

From first call to finished photographs

  1. 1A short call

    First, you answer a few questions in the Contact section below: how many photographs and of what, where they will appear, who will take care of styling. That way we talk specifics from the first minute.

    about 30 min

  2. 2Quote and deposit

    You receive an offer with a quote. Once you accept it, you pay a booking deposit: it holds the date for you and counts towards the price.

    offer usually within 24 h

  3. 3Shot plan

    I list the rooms and plan the day around the weather, the orientation of the building and the path of the sun.

    before the shoot

  4. 4The shoot

    I work room by room, at the pace the light sets. A frame is taken only once everything in it is where it belongs.

    half a day or a full day

  5. 5First look

    After a first selection and a quick edit, you receive a gallery. You choose the frames that go on to the final set.

    up to 5 working days

  6. 6Final edit and delivery

    The chosen frames get their final edit and retouching. They reach you with the licence, ready to work wherever we agreed.

    after you choose

How a quote comes together

You pay for what you actually need. The price comes mainly from my time and the licence: how long, where and for what you may use the photographs. I prepare every quote separately, from these seven elements.

Working time

Half a day or a full day of shooting, plus preparation, selection and editing — as much as the rooms and shots you want to show require.

Licence: non-exclusive or exclusive

I keep the copyright and you receive a licence. Non-exclusive is the standard: I may license the photographs to others as well. An exclusive licence, set out in writing, gives you certainty that within the agreed scope, period and territory no one else receives one.

Licence term

As long as you need: for an agreed period, or without a time limit.

Territory

Where you may use the photographs: in Poland, the European Union or worldwide, wherever your audience is.

Usage

What the photographs are for: website and social media, booking platforms, press and competitions, print, paid advertising. The licence covers exactly the uses we agree on.

Third parties

Anyone besides you who is to use the photographs — a furniture or lighting brand, a contractor, the architect, a publisher, a PR agency — receives a licence of their own. We usually settle this before the shoot.

Extras

Travel and transport, equipment (including flash with modifiers), styling or home staging, advanced retouching and, where needed, evening or seasonal shots. They appear in the quote only if you want them.

By default I may show the photographs in my portfolio, unless we agree otherwise — before an opening, for instance, or when the interior is private. Published photographs are credited to me.

Enquiry

Tell me about the place.

A few questions, a few minutes. It saves you time and lets me come to our call prepared.

About

I'm Piotr Bugaj, and I photograph interiors and architecture. Before photography, I studied the design of what fills interiors — furniture. It left me with an eye for proportion and material, but above all an attentiveness to what a space says and how it feels to be in it.

I learned photography from the inside: in fine-art printing, where every tonal step matters, and in the studio, working with light and digital capture. That's where I learned that good light can be patiently waited for — or built. I live in Warsaw and photograph across Poland and Europe.
